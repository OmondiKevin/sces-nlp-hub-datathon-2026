## 1. Purpose

This document records the source material, translation and localisation process, contribution and review metadata, attribution requirements and outstanding rights questions relating to Team 11's Dholuo sentiment dataset.

It distinguishes machine-translated material from human-localised material and does not present machine-translated sentences as originally human-authored.

## 4. Translation and contribution methods

All 300 Dholuo records derive from Sindhi source material.

Google Translate was used to translate the initial Sindhi sentences into Dholuo. The translated output was used largely as produced rather than being comprehensively rewritten or linguistically corrected by the team.

The team's clarified contribution breakdown is:

| Method | Records | Description |
|---|---:|---|
| Machine-translated | 276 | Google Translate output used largely as produced |
| Human-localised | 24 | Dholuo text human-localised from Sindhi material |
| **Total** | **300** | |

The dataset therefore contains predominantly machine-translated Dholuo text. It must not be described as 276 originally human-authored Dholuo records.

The final workbook and CSV metadata now correctly label 276 records as `machine_translated` and 24 as `human_localised`.

The sentiment categories are positive, negative and neutral. Translation does not guarantee that the original sentiment was preserved accurately in Dholuo.

## 7. Language quality and responsible handling

Machine translation may introduce grammatical errors, unnatural phrasing, incorrect sentiment, loss of idiomatic meaning and cultural or contextual mismatches.

The dataset should therefore be treated as experimental machine-translated Dholuo material, not as a linguistically validated corpus.

Further assessment by proficient Dholuo speakers is needed, particularly for:

- Naturalness and grammatical correctness.
- Negation and sentiment polarity.
- Idiomatic and culturally specific expressions.
- Ambiguous or mixed sentiment.
- Sarcasm and irony.
- Dialect variation and spelling.
- Contextual meaning and source-language influence.

The team must also inspect the final text for personal information or identifying details before publication.

## 8. AI and tool-use disclosure

Google Translate was used to translate the initial Sindhi source sentences into Dholuo.

The majority of the translated output was retained largely as produced. The remaining 24 records are classified as human-localised.

This process must be disclosed accurately. Machine-translated text must not be represented as originally human-authored or comprehensively human-validated.

Any additional AI tools used for generation, rewriting, classification, correction or review should also be disclosed based on the actual activities performed.

## 10. Publication and submission decision

The team should not claim that the source-derived dataset has unrestricted redistribution rights or that the machine-translated text has been linguistically validated.

Before publicly releasing the contribution CSV or source workbook, the team should resolve the applicable source licence and permission questions.

The datathon submission, repository code, documentation and demonstration should be considered separately from public redistribution of the underlying dataset. The team should confirm the organisers' requirements rather than assume that permission for one purpose covers all others.

If the rights position cannot be resolved before submission, the team should disclose the limitation and seek guidance from the organisers on the appropriate submission procedure.
