# Human-centred approach

Our motive was to let people express requests in familiar language. We collected their own wording for everyday situations and kept natural language mixing. This was language collection, not a formal study of people's access to AI.

We use Don Norman's Seven Stages of Action to explain the demo interaction:

| Stage | In Rada |
| --- | --- |
| Goal | A person wants to make a request, such as playing music |
| Intention | They decide to ask in their own words |
| Specify | They choose the wording to enter |
| Execute | They type it and click the button |
| Perceive | They see the prediction and simulated response |
| Interpret | They understand what Rada thinks they requested |
| Evaluate | They check whether that matches their meaning |

The last step checks recognition, not whether a real action happened. Plain action names, visible predictions and optional scores help explain the model. The interface is for assessment and matches the recorded demonstration.

This framework is our way of explaining the interaction; we do not claim it guided every development step. Formal usability testing remains future work.

Reference: Don Norman, The Design of Everyday Things, Chapter 2.
